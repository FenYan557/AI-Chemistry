## A B S T R A C T

A nitrogen-containing heterocyclic compound N-(3-(5-Mercapto-1H-tetrazol-1-yl) phenyl) acetamide (named L) was investigated as a new leveler to develop the microvias Cu <sup>fi</sup>lling with or without cracked plating in blind via. The synergistic effects of L with other plating additives were investigated by galvanostatic measurements (GMs) and cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements. The GMs show that the Δη values (Potential difference at different speeds of Cu rotating disk electrode) are approximately 24 mV which usually suggests better Cu <sup>fi</sup>lling performance for the corresponding electrolyte additives. In addition, the optical microscope (OM) measurements indicated that microvias cracked plating was more likely to occur when there is no accelerator in the copper plating solution. The results of OM experiments also show that the microvia super-<sup>fi</sup>lling would not appear although the copper plating rate at the bottom of the blind hole is higher than that of the outer copper layer of the blind hole if there is no air agitation in the copper plating solution. The <sup>fi</sup>nest copper electroplating formula made up of the virgin acid solution (VAS) only added the PEP, L, SPS, Cl<sup>−</sup> was found and applied to carry out super<sup>fi</sup>lling of microvias.

## 1. Introduction

Over recent years, the nano or micro-scale blind vias copper <sup>fi</sup>lling by electroplating has become one of the most practical methods for the fabrication of integrated circuit packaging substrates and circuit boards (PCBs) [1–6]. To achieve high-reliability interconnection by Cu <sup>fi</sup>lling blind holes between layers of electronic products is a long term and formidable task with high-density interconnection (HDI) technology. The super<sup>fi</sup>lling of blind holes is commonly regarded as an ef<sup>fi</sup>cient way and high performance with no void or seam during the microvia metallization process [7–8]. The super<sup>fi</sup>lling mode means the electrocrystallization rate speed of copper at the bottom of the blind via is much faster than that at the surface of the PCBs so called bottom-up deposition [9–10]. However, cracked plating that grows from the side and bottom of the blind hole penetrates the copper layer at the corners of the blind holes. It is often found as a serious fault in PCB industrial production, as shown in Fig. 1.

Cracked plating is an important reliability failure type of copper electroplating. It is manifested as copper cohesive cracks appearing at the corner locations of the microvias and will result in failures in electric and heat conductivity. The greater harm of the cracked plating phenomenon lies in its concealment. The conductivity of the copper layer for PCBs with cracked plating is usually the same as that of a normal printed circuit board at the early stages, so it is dif<sup>fi</sup>cult to be detected in electrical testing. However, some problems such as signal interruption and the sudden crash will occur and cause huge economic losses when the electronic devices and products smooth running for a period of time. Hence, there are urgent demands for developing processing guidelines for cracked plating that will lead to stable electroplating structures.

In order to ensure product quali<sup>fi</sup>ed reliability, many chemical additives have been applied in super<sup>fi</sup>lling process, including an accelerator (bis(3-sulfopropyl) disul<sup>fi</sup>de, SPS), a suppressor (polyethylene glycol, PEG or PEP(propylene oxide-ethylene oxide-propylene oxide)), and a leveler (Janus Green B, JGB) [11–15]. Among them, leveler ha important effects with regard to the asymmetric electroplating due to its capability of adsorbing at a high electric <sup>fi</sup>eld for the cathode sur face, which increase the overpotential of copper deposition and strongly inhibits copper deposition in high current density regions [16–18]. According to the literature [19,20], commonly used levelers for copper electroplating are organic compounds and their derivatives such as quaternary ammonium surfactants, dyestuff substances, nitrogen-containing heterocyclic compounds, and many others. The widely used levelers such as JGB, tricyclazole (TCA), 2-Mercaptopyridine, and 2-amino-4-methylbenzothiazole (AMBT) play an essential role in greatly hindering the deposition of Cu ion and smooth the surface of the deposited copper <sup>fi</sup>lm [21–28]. In consideration of electroplating additives degradation (such as macromolecular can be broken down into small molecular) in acid solution, and the distinct advantages for small molecules such as L (as shown in Fig. 2) with nitrogen-containing heterocyclic and the discovery has shown that no study has focused on this issue so far. The synergistic inhibition effect of L and other additives in plating solution were investigated by the different kinds of electrochemical methods.

## 2.1. The $Q / Q _ { O }$ values and Cl − concentrations dependence

A method to optimize the concentration of Cl<sup>−</sup> has been established based on cyclic voltammetry (CV) tests at a rotation speed of 1000 rpm, as shown in Fig. 3a. When the increase of Cl<sup>−</sup> in VAS containing SPS, PEP and $\mathbf { L } ,$ all of these CV plots indicate a clear synergistic inhibitory effect between $\mathrm { { C l } ^ { - } }$ and other additives. The phenomenon indicated that the copper (copper ions) became more and more dif<sup>fi</sup>cult to dissolve (deposit) due to the anchoring effect of Cl<sup>−</sup> leading to more PEP molecules adsorbing on the electrode surface [29–30]. In other words, the surface coverage(θ) values have been evaluated from the integral area values of Cu stripping peaks as a function of L concentrations based on the CV tests, as shown in Fig. 8. The adsorption of the leveler for Cu-RDE can affect the deposition and stripping rate of copper because of the physisorption or chemisorption process. The Langmuir adsorption equation of L on the Cu surface is given as Equation (1).

$$
\frac {Q}{Q _ {s}} = \theta = \frac {\mathrm{KC} _ {\mathrm{L}} ^ {n}}{1 + \mathrm{KC} _ {\mathrm{L}} ^ {n}}\tag{1}
$$

where $Q _ { s }$ represent the saturated Q value, θ represent surface coverage were determined as a function ofQ, K stand for adsorption equilibrium constant of $\mathrm { L } , \mathrm { C _ { L } }$ represent L concentration, and n stand for the kinetic order of L adsorption. $$
\frac {1}{Q} = \frac {1}{Q _ {s} K} \frac {1}{C _ {L} ^ {n}} + \frac {1}{Q _ {s}}\tag{2}

Instantaneous nucleation:

$$
\left(I / I _ {m}\right) ^ {2} = \frac {1 . 9 5 4 2}{t / t _ {m}} \{1 - \exp [ 1. 2 5 6 4 (t / t _ {m}) ] \} ^ {2}\tag{6}

$$

The active sites are activated simultaneously on the limited active electrodeposition surface for instantaneous nucleation, and nuclei rapidly form and slowly grow. In comparison, the number of formed nuclei increases rapidly during the deposition time for progressive nucleation. Therefore, the electrocrystallization surface can achieve a more uniform size through instantaneous nucleation. The synergistic effect of the gradient distribution of concentration results in the increased residual stress for the deposited copper <sup>fi</sup>lm and eventually forms a crack at the junction of the electroplated interfaces.

## 3. Conclusions

A new nitrogen-containing heterocyclic compound N-(3-(5-Mercapto-1H-tetrazol-1-yl) phenyl) acetamide was found as a leveler for microvias <sup>fi</sup>lling in HDI board. The $\Delta G _ { \mathrm { a d s } } ^ { 0 }$ shown that the L adsorption on the Cu surface was associated with mainly chemical adsorption processes. The optical microscope measurements indicated that the cracked plating was more likely to occur in a diagonal line across the bottom of the microvia at the corners of the blind holes when there is no accelerator in the copper plating solution. The thickness of deposited copper for the bottom of the microvia was larger than the outside without stirring, and this association may be attributed to PEP concentration non-uniform distribution on the sides and bottom of the microvia. Chronoamperometry tests indicate that the Cu electrodeposition nucleation could be considered as an instantaneous mechanism for the initial few seconds. An optimal copper plating solution composed of the virgin acid solution (VAS) only added the PEP, L, and SPS was found and applied to carry out super<sup>fi</sup>lling of microvias.

## 4. Experimental and methods

## 4.1. Electrochemical details

The electrochemical measurements were carried out with an elec trochemical workstation CHI 760E(Auto-Lab) equipped a standard three-electrode cell containing 250 mL electrolyte. A platinum rotating disk electrode (Pt-RDE) with a diameter of 5 mm was used as the work ing electrode (WE). The counter electrode (CE) was a copper bar placed in a hollow glass pipe <sup>fi</sup>lled with base electrolyte. Moreover, the reference electrode (RE) used the mercurous sulfate electrode (SMSE). All the potential values involved in this work are referred to SMSE scale. The galvanostatic measurements (GM) were used to investigate the adsorption of additives and evaluate the <sup>fi</sup>lling ability of the plating solution. Before GM tests, a layer of copper with the thickness of about 500 nm was pre-plated on the platinum rotating disk electrode for 5 min to transform Pt-RDE into Cu-RDE. During the galvanostatic measurements, the spinning speeds of the WE were taken at 100 and 1000 rpm to simulate the bottom and surface of the microvias, respectively. The electrolyte was controlled at a constant temperature of 298 K and the current density was $2 . 0 \ \mathrm { A } / \mathrm { d } \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 }$ . The inhibiting effect of copper plating was analyzed by cyclic voltammetry (CV) tests with a rotation speed of 1000 rpm. CV tests were conducted with the Pt-RDE as a working electrode and the scan rate is 5 mV/s while the frequency range is from 0.3 V to − 0.8 V. Chronoamperometry (CA) tests were conducted by the glassy carbon working electrode for a 3 mm diameter. All the experiments mentioned above must be carried out three times under the same conditions to ensure good repeatability.

## 4.2. Microvia copper electroplating

Electroplating experiments were performed in a 1500 mL Harring cell. The PCBs fragments by $\mathrm { C O } _ { 2 }$ laser ablation were used as a tested board for microvia <sup>fi</sup>lling experiments. The dimensions of the tested board with many microvias were 50 mm \* 150 mm. The diameter and depth of the blind microvias were 0.13 mm and 0.08 mm, respectively. Two parallel phosphorus-containing copper boards were used as the anode with the size of 5 cm \* 15 cm and placed at a distance of 20 cm in the Harring cell. To ensure better conductivity between the plated copper and the inner copper, smear of the microvias on the tested board were cleaned through a desmear process before the plating. The electroplating test was carried out at a room temperature of 298 K and the current density was kept at $2 . 0 \mathrm { A } / \mathrm { d } \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 }$ for 45 min. To enhance the mixing of the plating solution and good mass transfer, continuously <sup>fl</sup>owing air bubbles were applied to constantly agitate the plating solution throughout the whole electroplating process.

In the electroplating process, the virgin acid solution (VAS) included 0.88 M CuSO4 and 0.54 M $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } S 0 _ { 4 } .$ . Formulas of plating baths are $\mathtt { C l } ^ { - }$ from NaCl, PEP with a molecular weight of 2650 (BASF) employed as a suppressor, SPS used as an accelerator and L as studied leveler. The <sup>fi</sup>lling performance of microvias were observed by the cross-sections images using optical microscopy (OM). SEM pictures of the Cu electroplating surface were tested using a high vacuum at 15 keV by the Hitachi SU3500 SEM instrument. The Cu surface roughness was obtained by a digital surface roughness tester of TIME TR220.
